009                            ABSTRACT
010
011             This paper approaches the fundamental challenge of accelerating the inherently
012              autoregressive nature of sampling in Flow Matching (FM) models like Stable Dif-
013              fusion 3 and Flux through a numerical systems perspective. Specifically, we in-
014             troduce a unified framework that recasts the autoregressive sampling process as
                 solving a system of triangular nonlinear equations (TNEs), thereby facilitating a015
               paradigm shift toward non-autoregressive sampling featuring parallel vector field
016
                computation across multiple timesteps. Within this generic framework, we estab-
017
                    lish that: (1) the TNE system admits a unique solution corresponding precisely
018               to the autoregressive sampling trajectory; (2) solving the TNE system guaran-
019               tees convergence to this exact trajectory in far fewer sequential iterations. Build-
020              ing on these insights, we present ParaFlow, a training-free, step-parallel sampler
021               for accelerating autoregressive FM samplers. Extensive experiments validate that
022            ParaFlow achieves up to a 4ˆ reduction in sequential sampling steps and signifi-
023              cant wall-clock speedup of up to 4.3ˆ, with negligible impact on FID and CLIP
024               scores. The source code will be released publicly.

      1  INTRODUCTION027
028
       Flow Matching (FM) has emerged as a leading framework for high-fidelity generative model-
029
         ing, powering recent state-of-the-art systems such as Stable Diffusion 3 (Esser et al., 2024) and
030     Flux (Black Forest Labs, 2024). FM models learn a time-dependent vector field that transports a
031     simple prior distribution to the data distribution via an ordinary differential equation (ODE). Thus,
032     sampling reduces to numerically integrating this ODE, an inherently autoregressive procedure that
033     requires tens of sequential evaluations of a large neural network, constituting a major bottleneck for
034     real-time and interactive use.
035    Two main strategies have been explored to mitigate this cost. The first develops more efficient
036     numerical solvers: high-order methods such as DPM-Solver (Lu et al., 2022a;b) and task-specific
037     solvers (Shaul et al., 2023; Frankel et al., 2025) reduce the number of function evaluations (NFEs),
038     enabling generation in fewer steps, but remain fully sequential. The second pursues model distilla-
039      tion (Salimans & Ho, 2022), compressing multi-step teachers into student models capable of few-
040     shot or even one-shot generation. Approaches such as Latent Consistency Models (LCMs) (Luo
041      et al., 2023), based on Consistency Models (Song et al., 2023), achieve strong results but require
        expensive retraining and degrade fidelity. Both methods reduce the step count but do not remove042
         sequential dependencies.
043
044     In contrast to these approaches, our work departs from the sequential paradigm. We propose
045    ParaFlow, a framework that accelerates sampling not by reducing steps but by executing them
046      in parallel. Our key idea is to recast the entire sampling trajectory—ordinarily produced by an ODE
       solver—as a system of Triangular Nonlinear Equations (TNEs). This reformulation decouples de-047
        pendencies across timesteps, enabling simultaneous evaluation of the vector field at multiple steps.
048
049   We show that the proposed TNE system admits a unique solution mathematically identical to the
050      trajectory of the original autoregressive sampler, ensuring no loss in sample quality. To solve this
        system efficiently, we adopt a fixed-point iteration scheme that converges rapidly to the exact so-051
         lution. A sliding-window implementation further makes the approach computationally feasible for
052
        long sampling chains.
053
       Our main contributions are:

066    2  RELATED WORK
067
068     Accelerating generative model sampling is a long-standing research problem. For diffusion and
069     flow-based models, which rely on iterative refinement, this is particularly critical.
070    Numerical ODE Solvers for Flow Matching. Flow Matching (FM) (Lipman et al., 2022; Liu et al.,
071     2022; Chen & Lipman, 2024) frames the sampling process as the integration of a learned time-
072     dependent vector field. Early work (Song & Ermon, 2020; Song et al., 2021) in score-based SDEs
073    and their corresponding probability-flow ODEs enabled deterministic sampling and efficient likeli-
074    hood evaluation. FM further refines this by training continuous normalizing flows (Chen et al., 2018)
075     through conditional vector field regression along optimized probability paths, such as those (e.g.
076    Lipman et al., 2022; Tong et al., 2023; Lipman et al., 2024; Schusterbauer et al., 2025; Wang et al.,
077     2025) derived from diffusion or optimal transport, improving stability and speed with standard ODE
         solvers.078
079    The choice of numerical integrator is crucial. First-order methods, such as Euler and DDIM (Song
080      et al., 2020), are computationally inexpensive but require a large number of steps to achieve high-
081     quality results. To mitigate this problem, second-order solvers, such as Heun’s method, are fre-
        quently employed (Karras et al., 2022). For the most demanding tasks, higher-order solvers tai-082
         lored specifically for diffusion models, such as the DPM-Solver family (Lu et al., 2022a;b; Zheng
083
          et al., 2023) and Bespoke Solvers (Shaul et al., 2023; 2024), offer improved sample quality with
084
        lower NFEs. Furthermore, methods like S4S (Frankel et al., 2025), AYS (Sabour et al., 2024) and
085   DMN (Xue et al., 2024) have demonstrated potential for reducing discretization and global error.
086     However, these methods still require strictly sequential execution, limiting opportunities for paral-
087      lelization.
088
        Parallel Sampling.  ParaDiGMS (Shih et al., 2024) accelerates sampling by leveraging paral-
089      lelized Picard iterations to simultaneously predict and iteratively refine future denoising steps.
090    ParaTAA (Tang et al., 2024) reformulates the autoregressive diffusion sampling process as TNEs
091    and uses fixed-point methods to solve multiple steps in parallel. ParaSolver (Lu et al., 2025) fur-
092      ther models the sampling process as a system of banded nonlinear equations, and shows how to
093     exploit structural properties and hierarchical initialization to preserve sample quality while greatly
094     reducing inference time. Our proposed method, ParaFlow, builds on the parallel solving framework
095     pioneered by those works, but is the first to (i) systematically formulate parallel sampling in the
096     context of Flow Matching (FM) models; (ii) provide accompanying theoretical guarantees specific
097      to FM-based parallel solving; and (iii) empirically validate that TNE-based parallel sampling works
         for state-of-the-art FM models without degradation in sample fidelity.098

471     This paper introduces ParaFlow, a novel framework that reimagines the sampling process for Flow
472     Matching models. By reformulating the inherently autoregressive ODE solving process as a system
473     of triangular nonlinear equations, ParaFlow enables full step-level parallelism, allowing multiple
474     sampling steps to be computed simultaneously. Our theoretical analysis confirms that this method
475     converges to the exact trajectory of a standard sequential sampler. Empirically, on state-of-the-art
       models like Stable Diffusion 3 and Flux, ParaFlow delivers wall-clock speedups of up to 4.3ˆ with476
         negligible impact on generation quality. This work opens a new avenue for accelerating genera-
