      Abstract. Accurate 3D tumor segmentation is vital for clinical decision-
      making, but pixel-wise annotation is expensive. Mixed-supervised learn-
       ing combines a small set of mask labels with abundant box labels, yet
       reconstruction-kernel heterogeneity creates large domain gaps. We pro-
      pose WCC4MS, a jointly optimized lesion-centered framework that mod-
        els kernel-related frequency shifts via a 3D discrete wavelet transform.
     The input volume  is decomposed into low- and high-frequency com-
       ponents, and the scaled high-frequency coefficients generate synthetic
      smooth and sharp variants. A dual-branch architecture learns from mask
      and box annotations with task-specific decoders, while a cross-branch
      KL-divergence constraint enforces semantic consistency. An auxiliary
      domain-aware head encourages quality-sensitive features and improves
       robustness to domain shift. At inference, only the segmentation branch is
      used on candidate-centered patches; the box branch is used only during
       training to exploit weak annotations. Experiments across three mask-
      annotated CT datasets and one box-only dataset show consistent gains
       in segmentation accuracy and cross-domain generalization compared to
       prior mixed- and weakly supervised methods.

     Keywords: Mixed-supervised segmentation · Cross-domain consistency
           · 3D wavelet transform

lead to a significant performance drop in clinical applications. Moreover, sim-
ply integrating existing domain adaptation methods into a mixed supervision
framework is non-trivial. Image quality assessment and segmentation represent
fundamentally distinct objectives: the former focuses on identifying shifts in data
distribution, while the latter prioritizes precise tumor edge classification [4]. This
fundamental difference poses challenges to existing mix-supervised frameworks.
   In this paper, we present WCC4MS, a simple yet effective cost-minimization
framework for 3D tumor segmentation in a mixed-supervised setting. Our dual-
branch architecture handles mask and box via annotation-type routing and task-
specific branches. The framework focuses on lesion-centered segmentation af-
ter candidate localization, which is consistent with common 3D CAD pipelines
where suspicious regions are first localized and then refined by a lesion-level
segmentation model. CT data exhibit significant domain variations due to re-
construction kernels, with high-noise versions preserving sharper edges than
low-noise counterparts. Inspired by multi-kernel CT analysis [16], we apply 3D
wavelet transforms [42] to decompose input volumes into low-frequency (LF) and
high-frequency (HF) components. To approximate kernel-related frequency shifts
without raw metadata, we scale the HF components to generate smooth and
sharp variants. To enhance robustness against such heterogeneity, a consistency
constraint is imposed between the two branches. Furthermore, we introduce a
domain classification branch to explicitly predict the data provenance, guiding
the encoder to learn quality-aware features and thereby improving cross-domain
generalizability and structural consistency.
    It is worth emphasizing that our method differs fundamentally from the foun-
dation models [12,21]. While foundation models aim for universal diagnostic ca-
pability and rely on massive data and computational resources across multiple
anatomical regions, our approach focuses on maximizing the use of available su-
pervision within limited datasets and computational budgets, making it more
practical for clinical deployment. Our contributions are highlighted as follows:

 • We propose WCC4MS, a novel mixed-supervised framework for 3D tumor
    segmentation that unifies mask-supervised and box-supervised learning in a
    dual-branch architecture.
 • We introduce wavelet transforms to model domain shifts and effectively
    bridge image quality assessment with tumor segmentation tasks to enhance
    the cross-domain adaptability of the model.
 • We conduct extensive experiments on three typical tumor segmentation
    datasets with one auxiliary box dataset. Our method outperforms state-
    of-the-art approaches.

2  Related Work

2.1  Medical Mixed-supervised Segmentation

Though stronger labels lead to better segmentations, practical clinical deploy-
ment necessitates label-efficient solutions. Traditional approaches often treat dif-
ferent supervision types independently. In contrast, mixed-supervised methods

=== PDF PAGE 4 ===
4      T. Lan et al.


can leverage massive, low-cost, and weak labels, as well as a few dense labels,
to ensure segmentation accuracy. Specifically, recent works address this by de-
coupling supervision branches, where a basic feature extractor is shared [17,27].
However, treating these independent branches equally may introduce a high risk
of overfitting towards weakly supervised data. Recent semi-supervised medical
segmentation methods also explore teacher-student interactions to improve la-
bel efficiency, such as DualFete [39], which revisits feedback between teacher and
student models. Different from these semi-supervised settings, our work focuses
on mixed supervision with heterogeneous mask and box annotations collected
from different domains.


2.2  Domain Generalization in Medical Imaging

Different supervision signals typically originate from distinct data sources, and
their simultaneous use may introduce domain shift issues. Domain generaliza-
tion (DG) aims to learn a model from multiple source domains that can di-
rectly generalize to unseen target domains [36,44]. Compared to domain adap-
tation, DG is better suited for medical imaging problems due to the low in-
teroperability and scarcity of sample diversity characteristic of medical image
data [10]. Several approaches focus on learning domain-invariant features by
reducing distribution differences between source domains [6,26,38]. Alternative
meta-learning strategies simulate domain shifts through episodic training, where
models are alternately exposed to meta-train and meta-test splits during opti-
mization [9,13]. Current mixed-supervised models overlook the effects of domain
shift on generalization. Our wavelet-based framework decomposes CT images
into multi-frequency components, explicitly modeling acquisition variations to
improve noise robustness.


2.3  Multi-Task Learning

Multi-task learning has proven to be a practical approach for training general-
ized models that can address multiple objectives simultaneously. Such joint op-
timization across spatial and scale domains has shown benefits in diverse vision
tasks [41]. In the medical domain, it has been widely adopted to boost diagnos-
tic performance by jointly learning related tasks, such as capturing inter-disease
relationships and leveraging auxiliary information [18,29]. Beyond medical imag-
ing, structure-enhanced correlation modeling has also benefited other multi-task
scenarios [15]. Despite its success, a critical aspect often remains underexplored:
the role of image quality in medical diagnosis. While some prior work has at-
tempted to include quality assessment as an auxiliary task, they reported limited
improvements due to the lack of an explicit mechanism to leverage quality in-
formation effectively [27]. In contrast, WCC4MS introduces a framework that
explicitly integrates domain knowledge into the diagnostic process, unlocking its
potential to enhance model accuracy and robustness.

3  Method

3.1  Overview

We focus on maximizing the utilization of multi-datasets with diverse label cate-
gories by leveraging the advantages of a dual-branch network to enhance general-
ization against domain shift. Unlike existing implicitly auxiliary learning [4], we
leverage the deterministic information provided by bounding boxes, specifically
their positions and sizes, to explicitly enhance the sensitivity of the network to
segmentation regions. Figure 2 shows the architecture of the WCC4MS, a frame-
work specifically designed for domain shift in the mixed supervised paradigm.


3.2  KernelWave

The reconstruction kernel is a crucial parameter contributing to domain shifts in
CT imaging [16]. It operates as frequency-domain filtering, mathematically char-
acterized via the Fourier transform as the selective amplification or suppression
of specific frequency components within CT images. However, due to patient pri-
vacy concerns, public datasets typically do not retain raw metadata, making it
impossible to unify CT data distributions through the inverse Fourier transform.
As a widely employed multiscale frequency-domain tool, wavelet transformation
enables decomposition of CT images acquired with unknown kernels into distinct
subbands. Adjusting high-frequency subband coefficients enables the generation

4  Experiments

4.1  Datasets

In our experiments, we split each dataset with masks into training and test
sets with a 4:1 ratio. The PN9, which contains only box annotations, is used
exclusively to provide auxiliary training and is not involved in evaluation.

 • LIDC-IDRI: The LIDC-IDRI [2] dataset comprises lung cancer screening
    thoracic CT scans with marked-up annotated tumors. Following the previous
   work [29], we include only CT scans that meet the LUNA16 selection criteria,
    resulting in 586 CT scans with a total of 1,131 nodules.
 • MSD-Lung: The MSD-Lung is part of the MSD [1] collection. It contains 3D
  CT scans from 63 patients.
 • LNDb: LNDb is a dataset comprising 294 lung CT scans [25]. The database
    comprises 1429 unique findings by five radiologists.
 • PN9: The CT scans in PN9 are obtained by a series of CT manufacturers
   and corresponding models, which are multidomain [23]. It contains 8798 CT
    scans with 40439 bounding boxes for nodules.

  We focus on lung tumor segmentation for two key reasons: (1) The abun-
dance of publicly available lung CT datasets enables robust cross-validation and
statistical power; (2) Lung tumors exhibit high heterogeneity in size (3–95mm),
morphology (spiculated/lobulated), and density (GGN/solid), making them a
representative benchmark for mixed-supervision learning in medical imaging.

Contribution of the Key Components We conduct ablation experiments

shifts is important for cross-domain adaptability.
   Since disabling AD also removes the mechanism that uses box supervision,
we additionally perform an AD-fixed controlled check to isolate KW/KL/DC.

to 1.54 mm. Removing DC decreases cross-domain DSC by 1.6%, supporting its
role as a domain-aware regularizer rather than a redundant classifier.

Mechanism-level validation of KernelWave Although KernelWave is mo-
tivated by CT reconstruction-kernel variation, it is not intended to exactly sim-
ulate the reconstruction process. Instead, it serves as a metadata-free frequency
proxy that approximates the dominant kernel-related effect, i.e., high-frequency
amplification or suppression. To verify whether this proxy is consistent with real
multi-kernel CT data, we analyze RKPN, a paired B31f/B60f lung CT dataset
reconstructed with smooth and sharp kernels. Since RKPN provides detection
annotations rather than voxel-wise masks, we use it only for mechanism-level
frequency validation, not as an additional segmentation benchmark.
   As shown in Table 8, KernelWave-induced sharp variants increase high-
frequency energy in the same direction as real B31f/B60f reconstruction differ-
ences. The high-frequency energy ratio is 1.50 for KernelWave-sharp/original and
1.44 for real B60f/B31f. The spectral correlation between real and KernelWave-
induced frequency changes reaches r = 0.71, indicating that KernelWave cap-
tures kernel-related frequency shifts rather than arbitrary perturbations. In addi-
tion, a five-seed comparison on LIDC-IDRI shows that KernelWave significantly
outperforms Gaussian blur, further supporting the benefit of wavelet-based high-
frequency modulation over generic smoothing.

5  Conclusion

We propose a wavelet-driven mixed-supervised framework for cross-domain 3D
tumor segmentation. By integrating masks and boxes through a Dual-Branch
Supervision module with cross-branch consistency, KernelWave enables domain-
aware representation learning that improves robustness to CT reconstruction
variations. Experiments across multiple datasets demonstrate superior perfor-
mance under varied annotation ratios. Future work will extend the framework
to other modalities and weaker annotation regimes.
